What this model is

A Bayesian linear regression explaining the month-to-month change in the PPP deviation (tab 1) from four candidate channels — carry, fiscal risk (Brazil 5Y CDS), the breakeven de-anchoring gap (breakeven − CMN target), and the dollar index (DXY) — entered contemporaneously (Δchannel(t), no extra lag), matching the timing choice in the "State-Space (Attempt Two)" tab. Rebuilt 2026-07-28 around this not-lagged channel set at direct user request, replacing an earlier lagged carry/terms-of-trade/raw-breakeven/fiscal version — that version's design writeup, results, and the cross-spec/posterior-histogram sections it supported are in models/bayesian_deviation_model.md for reference; its saved traces remain on disk but are no longer read by this tab.

1 · Dependent variable

D(t) = 100 · ln( ptax(t) / equilibrium(t) )

The PPP deviation from tab 1 (any base month gives identical results here — only the intercept shifts, see the .md's derivation).

2 · Why first differences

ADF + KPSS (2026-07-23) on D(t), carry → I(1)
breakeven, fiscal → I(0) — not retested for dxy/gap

Pre-registered rule from the original spec: difference everything uniformly rather than mix levels and differences. Kept as the conservative default here too, though dxy and the de-anchoring gap haven't had their own ADF/KPSS run.

3 · Regression equation

Δdev(t) = α + β₁Δcarry(t) + β₂Δcds(t)
+ β₃Δbreakeven_gap(t) + β₄Δdxy(t) + ε(t)

All four regressors standardized (mean 0, sd 1) before fitting. Contemporaneous timing (t, not t−1) — the original spec's extra one-month lag is dropped here.

4 · Priors & likelihood

α ~ Normal(0,10)  β ~ Normal(0,2)
σ ~ HalfNormal(5)  ε ~ Normal(0,σ)

Weakly-informative priors on standardized regressors. Normal errors only for this spec — no Student-t comparison was run.

What this model is

"Attempt two" — a Bayesian fit of the PPP deviation's own dynamics, with Y = the nominal exchange rate (ptax), not REER. Equilibrium's own noise (η) is fixed at zero, so equilibrium(t) is exactly the PPP path from tab 1 (not filtered) and deviation(t) is directly observed, not latent — this is a one-equation Bayesian AR(1)-with-regressors fit, not yet a true two-state Kalman filter. Two deliberate departures from the Bayesian Model tab: deviation enters as a level regressed on its own lag (φ), and the five channels enter contemporaneously (no extra one-month lag) — that tab's regressors are lagged by one additional month, which this model argues works against how financial variables actually move. Dollar Index (DXY) added as a 5th channel (2026-07-24) — a global dollar-strength proxy alongside the four bilateral BRL channels. Full design notes: analytics/exchange_rate/CLAUDE.md, "State-space equilibrium model" section.

1 · Measurement

ptax(t) = equilibrium(t) · exp( deviation(t) / 100 )

Same deviation definition as tab 1 — 100·ln(ptax/equilibrium) — just rearranged to solve for the nominal rate.

2 · Transition (η = 0)

equilibrium(t) = equilibrium(t−1) + π_diff(t)

Fixed at zero noise for this first build — equilibrium is exactly the relative-PPP path, not filtered against the observed rate. Freeing η is the concrete next step (see callout below).

3 · Deviation

deviation(t) = α + φ·deviation(t−1)
+ Σ βᵢ·z(Δchannelᵢ(t)) + ε(t)

φ bounded to (0,1) via a Uniform prior — imposes stationarity a priori. deviation(t−1) kept in native units; the five channel deltas standardized.

4 · Priors & likelihood

α ~ Normal(0,10)  φ ~ Uniform(0,1)
β ~ Normal(0,2)  σ ~ HalfNormal(5)

Same weakly-informative prior scales as the Bayesian Model tab, for comparability.

Next step, not yet attempted: let η be freely estimated instead of fixed at zero — only then does equilibrium genuinely blend the raw PPP signal with what the observed rate implies, making this a real two-state Kalman filter rather than a single regression on an already-observed series.

What this model is

The real two-state Kalman filter — η (equilibrium's own noise) is now freely estimated, replacing the "State-Space (Attempt Two)" tab's η=0 shortcut. Measurement is still exact (y(t) = eq(t) + dev(t), no separate observation noise): equilibrium and deviation are only separately identified because their dynamics differ — equilibrium is a pure random walk driven by the actual BR−US inflation differential; deviation is a mean-reverting AR(1) driven by the four channels. A direct consequence: since the measurement is exact, the filtered eq(t) and dev(t) sum EXACTLY to the actual rate on every posterior draw (verified to ~1e-14) — they're mirror images of each other, not independently "fitted." The informative comparison is therefore how the filter's split between them differs from the rigid η=0 split, not a fitted-vs-actual chart (which would be a tautology here).

1 · Measurement (exact)

y(t) = eq(t) + dev(t)

y(t) = 100·ln(ptax(t)). No separate observation noise — R=0 in Kalman-filter terms.

2 · Transition (η free)

eq(t) = eq(t−1) + π_diff(t) + η(t)
η(t) ~ N(0, σ_η²)

π_diff(t) is the real, observed BR−US monthly log inflation differential — not a stand-in. σ_η is now a free parameter instead of fixed at 0.

3 · Deviation (same form)

dev(t) = α + φ·dev(t−1)
+ Σ βᵢ·z(Δchannelᵢ(t)) + ε(t)

Identical to the η=0 tab's deviation equation — same channels, same contemporaneous timing, same priors on α/φ/β.

4 · Estimation

Hand-rolled Kalman filter via pytensor.scan
log-likelihood added via pm.Potential

No ready-made state-space package covers this exact shape (degenerate measurement noise, exogenous drivers in both equations); validated against the η=0 baseline first (σ_η→0 reproduces it to ~1e-9).

What this model is

A classic BEER-style ("Behavioral Equilibrium Exchange Rate") specification, user-specified 2026-07-24: the PPP deviation is explained by the contemporaneous LEVEL of seven pull factors at once, not their month-over-month change (the other tabs' approach) and with no autoregressive term at all — no φ, no dependence on last month's deviation. A plain static multivariate regression: deviation(t) = α + Σ βᵢ·z(channelᵢ(t)) + ε(t). Two channels are new here — trade balance and current account, both % of GDP (12m-trailing-sum flow over 12m-trailing-sum GDP-in-USD, same convention as the Panorama Cambial report's own "% PIB" toggle) — added specifically for this model after a delta-form version of each showed no signal in the other tabs' AR(1) spec.

1 · Measurement

ptax(t) = equilibrium(t) · exp( deviation(t) / 100 )

Same deviation definition as every other tab — 100·ln(ptax/equilibrium).

2 · Pull factors (levels)

deviation(t) = α + Σ βᵢ·z(channelᵢ(t)) + ε(t)

Seven channels, each its own standardized LEVEL at time t: carry, terms-of-trade, breakeven, fiscal risk, dollar index (DXY), trade balance %GDP, current account %GDP.

3 · No persistence term

(no deviation(t−1), no φ)

Unlike the other two model tabs, this spec has no mean-reversion mechanism of its own — whatever the 7 channels don't explain is pure residual, not "yet to revert." No half-life concept applies here.

4 · Priors & likelihood

α ~ Normal(0,10)  β ~ Normal(0,2)
σ ~ HalfNormal(5)

α is an addition beyond the literal equation as specified (which has no constant) — kept so the model isn't forced to assume zero unconditional mean deviation.

Read the coefficients as descriptive, not causal: with no autoregressive term and no explicit time trend, a levels-on-levels regression like this one can't distinguish a genuine pull-factor relationship from two persistent series that simply trended together over the same 18-year window — the classic spurious-regression risk, sharper here than in the other tabs' delta-based specs. Trade balance and current account are themselves highly correlated (one is a large component of the other), so their individual coefficients — even where both come out significant — should be read as "the model can't fully separate these two," not as two independent, clean effects.

What this tab is

The BEER model, narrowed to its "core 4" channels — carry, breakeven, fiscal, dollar index (DXY) — after terms-of-trade, trade balance, and current account were each tested and dropped in turn (tot never showed signal; current account's sign flipped once trade balance was removed, revealing the two were fighting over shared, collinear variance; trade balance itself was then dropped too, by direct request, to keep this reduced spec focused on the four channels that held up most consistently). This tab asks a question the single whole-sample fit can't answer on its own: are these coefficients actually stable over time, or is the whole-sample estimate just an average over regimes that don't agree?
Rolling estimates below use a 60-month (5-year) window, stepped one month at a time, re-estimated by OLS with HAC standard errors (maxlags=3) — not a full Bayesian re-fit per window, which would take much longer for negligible extra insight here. Each channel is standardized using the FULL SAMPLE's own mean/std (not re-standardized per window), so a beta's apparent drift reflects a real change in the estimated relationship, not just the window's own shifting mean/std.

What this tab is

Turns qualitative FX commentary from asset-manager monthly letters into a numeric time series: for each month, how much of the narrative attributes BRL/USD moves to each of nine fixed causal regimes — fiscal (Brasil), monetary/carry, politics (Brasil), global USD, commodities/terms of trade/external accounts, global risk sentiment, China/EM growth, trade policy, and capital flows/positioning. This is a manual-extraction pilot, not an automated text-to-claim pipeline — each manager's claims are hand-extracted from source letters following a fixed taxonomy and rule set (see analytics/exchange_rate/models/fx_attribution_model.md).
Sign convention: direction scores the claim's implied effect on BRL, never on the claim's own subject — +1 strongly BRL-appreciation-supportive, −1 strongly BRL-depreciation-driving. A claim that "the dollar is strengthening globally" is scored negative (bad for BRL), not positive. Each manager's corpus is scoped independently (coverage windows and document counts differ) — cross-manager comparisons should account for that rather than reading a lower claim count as "this manager talks about FX less."

What this tab is

Estimated by Ridge (L2 penalty) instead of OLS or a Bayesian fit, and re-estimated on a rolling window like the BEER model's own rolling tab. Grew from 2 channels up to 8 across several rounds (fiscal, DXY, four carry variants, breakeven gap, terms of trade), then shrunk back down to 4 — fiscal risk (CDS), carry-to-vol, the dollar index (DXY), and BR yield-curve steepening (10Y-2Y) — once a walk-forward out-of-sample test showed this smaller set matches the 8-channel version's whole-sample fit almost exactly while predicting slightly BETTER out-of-sample. Fewer parameters, same signal.
Dependent variable changed from PPP deviation to the exchange rate's own return. Every earlier version of this tab regressed Δdeviation — the change in how far PTAX sits from its PPP-implied equilibrium — which forces a coefficient of exactly 1 on PPP's own implied move (it's subtracted out before anything is estimated). This version regresses Δfx(t) = the exchange rate's own log return directly, with PPP entering (briefly) as one more freely-estimated channel instead of an assumed equilibrium. That channel's coefficient came out unstable across rolling windows (whole-sample +0.34, rolling mean +0.04, swinging from −0.86 to +1.48) — dropping it entirely cost almost nothing in-sample and genuinely improved out-of-sample prediction, so PPP is excluded here and α absorbs its average drift instead, same as every channel this tab has dropped for the same reason.
Curve steepening (10Y-2Y) is new — the BR nominal pre-fixed curve's 10-year rate minus its 2-year rate, an alternate, market-based proxy for fiscal risk alongside CDS: when markets price rising long-run fiscal risk (debt-sustainability or monetization concerns), long-dated rates tend to move more than short, policy-anchored ones, steepening the curve independently of whether 5y CDS itself moves. It came in with the same sign as fiscal risk itself — a steeper curve pushes the exchange rate the same direction a wider CDS spread does.
Includes a single AR(1) term — Δfx(t-1) itself, kept in raw units alongside the z-scored channels — so last month's move in the exchange rate has a direct, persistent pull on this month's, independent of any channel. An earlier version of this tab instead gave every channel its own 6-month lag structure (48 parameters); a walk-forward out-of-sample test showed that version overfits — one-step-ahead error got WORSE with every added lag, even though the in-sample R² kept climbing — while this single shared AR(1) term, at the cost of one extra parameter, genuinely improves out-of-sample prediction. The φ coefficient on the rolling-coefficient chart below is small and negative: a month that moves more than usual tends to see a modest partial reversal the next month.
"Baseline" on the decomposition chart below combines three things that aren't one of the four economic channels: the anchor rate (where the bridge starts), α (the constant drift), and the AR(1) term (the exchange rate's own momentum). None of the three is a fundamental driver on its own, so rather than three separate bars cluttering the chart, they're shown as one combined bucket — everything the model attributes to its own structure/mechanics, as opposed to fiscal, carry/vol, DXY, or curve steepening.
λ is not fixed by hand — it's chosen by walk-forward temporal cross-validation: fit on an expanding window, score one step ahead (never used to fit that step), walk forward across the whole history, and keep the λ with the best average out-of-sample squared error. The chart below shows that selection directly.
Coefficients are then re-estimated on a rolling 60-month window (same window the BEER "Rolling Window (Core 4)" tab uses) at that fixed λ — Ridge alone stabilizes correlated channels' coefficients, it doesn't handle regime change on its own; a single whole-sample Ridge fit would still describe one average relationship across calm and crisis periods.
