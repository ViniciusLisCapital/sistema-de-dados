What this tab is

Turns qualitative FX commentary from asset-manager monthly letters into a numeric time series: for each month, how much of the narrative attributes BRL/USD moves to each of nine fixed causal regimes — fiscal (Brasil), monetary/carry, politics (Brasil), global USD, commodities/terms of trade/external accounts, global risk sentiment, China/EM growth, trade policy, and capital flows/positioning. This is a manual-extraction pilot, not an automated text-to-claim pipeline — each manager's claims are hand-extracted from source letters following a fixed taxonomy and rule set (see analytics/exchange_rate/models/fx_attribution_model.md).
Sign convention: direction scores the claim's implied effect on BRL, never on the claim's own subject — +1 strongly BRL-appreciation-supportive, −1 strongly BRL-depreciation-driving. A claim that "the dollar is strengthening globally" is scored negative (bad for BRL), not positive. Each manager's corpus is scoped independently (coverage windows and document counts differ) — cross-manager comparisons should account for that rather than reading a lower claim count as "this manager talks about FX less."

What this tab is

The model: Δfx(t) = α + φ·Δfx(t-1) + βfiscal·Δfiscal(t) + βcarry/vol·Δcarry_vol(t) + βdxy·Δdxy(t) + βsteep·Δcurve_steep(t) + ε(t) — the exchange rate's own month-over-month log return, regressed on four economic channels plus its own one-month lag, estimated by Ridge (L2 penalty) instead of OLS or a Bayesian fit. λ is chosen by walk-forward temporal cross-validation (fit on an expanding window, score one step ahead, keep the λ with the best average out-of-sample error), and coefficients are re-estimated on a rolling 60-month window so they can track regime change instead of describing one average relationship across calm and crisis periods.

Fiscal — Brazil's 5-year sovereign CDS (USD), a direct market price of default/fiscal risk. Positive coefficient: rising fiscal risk pushes the exchange rate the same direction as a depreciation.

Carry / FX vol — the BR-US policy rate differential divided by BRL's own trailing 6-month realized volatility, a risk-adjusted carry-attractiveness measure. Negative coefficient here — the same "wrong sign vs. simple UIP" finding this project's other carry-based models keep landing on.

Dollar index (DXY) — a global, not Brazil-specific, dollar-strength gauge. Positive coefficient: broad dollar strength depreciates BRL alongside every other EM currency, independent of anything specific to Brazil.

Curve steepening (10Y-2Y) — the BR nominal pre-fixed curve's 10-year rate minus its 2-year rate, an alternate, market-based fiscal-risk proxy alongside CDS: when markets price rising long-run fiscal risk, long-dated rates tend to move more than short, policy-anchored ones. Same sign as fiscal risk itself.

AR(1) — Δfx(t-1) itself, kept in raw units (not z-scored). Small and negative: a month that moves more than usual tends to see a modest partial reversal the next month. Replaced an earlier version of this tab that instead gave every channel its own 6-month lag structure (48 parameters) — a walk-forward test showed that version overfits (out-of-sample error got worse with every added lag), while this single shared term, at the cost of one extra parameter, genuinely improves out-of-sample prediction.

Baseline, on the decomposition chart below, combines three things that aren't one of the four economic channels: the anchor rate (where the bridge starts), α (the constant drift), and the AR(1) term (the exchange rate's own momentum). None of the three is a fundamental driver on its own, so they're shown as one bucket rather than three separate bars.

PPP itself was tested here too — as a freely-estimated channel (the BR-US relative inflation differential), not as an assumed equilibrium the exchange rate is measured against. Its coefficient came out unstable across rolling windows (whole-sample +0.34, rolling mean +0.04, swinging from −0.86 to +1.48) — dropping it cost almost nothing in-sample and genuinely improved out-of-sample prediction, so it's excluded and α absorbs its average drift instead, same as every channel this tab has dropped for the same reason.
