# Administered (Monitored) Prices

**Type:** Price group / source of inflation shocks
**Tags:** #administered-prices #relative-prices #indexation #supply-shocks #ipca #brazil

---

## Definition

In the Brazilian IPCA, prices "administered by contract or monitored" are those little affected by domestic supply and demand conditions or regulated in some way by a public agency — oil by-products, fixed telephone, residential electricity and public transportation. The group was defined by the Copom in July 2001; the rest of the index is "market" (free) prices (Minella et al., 2002).

## Mechanism

Minella et al. give three features that make administered prices behave differently from market prices:
- **International prices**: oil by-products follow oil prices in domestic currency.
- **Higher exchange-rate pass-through**: oil prices move with the exchange rate, electricity rates are partly linked to it, and contracts for electricity and telephone rates are partly indexed to the IGP, a general price index more exposed to the exchange rate than consumer indexes.
- **Stronger backward-looking behavior**: annual contractual adjustments based on past IGP and exchange-rate changes.

The first and third are outside monetary policy's control and the second only partly within it. Backward-looking indexation makes the inflationary effect of any shock more persistent and disinflation more costly in output, especially with declining targets.

**The policy response.** Monetary policy should offset only the secondary effects of a relative-price shock and preserve the initial realignment. The BCB therefore measures the shock's "primary effect": administered inflation above the target, weighted by the group's share of the IPCA, minus the parts explained by inertia from the previous year and by the exchange rate — since policy is responsible for offsetting inertia and the exchange rate partly reflects policy and demand. The target is then adjusted by this primary effect (and part of the inherited inertia) before being compared with the inflation forecast.

## When it applies / when it fails

- The decomposition assumes the same inertia and pass-through for all goods; in practice some contracts follow the 12-month exchange rate change and others (gasoline) the previous month's (Minella et al.).
- Accommodating first-round effects is a trade-off between flexibility and credibility when the forecast approaches the upper band limit; the authors say there is "no clear answer", and that more flexibility is warranted the higher the bank's credibility, the better its communication and the larger the output cost of returning to target.
- Excluding administered items from the target index is the obvious alternative and is rejected in Brazil (see [[core_inflation]]).

## BRL / application to the current analysis

The concept is Brazilian. Minella et al. (2002) report that administered prices rose systematically above market prices from mid-1995 and were a main source of the 2001–2002 target breaches, together with the exchange rate.
