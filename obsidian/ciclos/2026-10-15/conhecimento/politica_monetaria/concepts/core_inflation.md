# Core Inflation

**Type:** Inflation measure / choice of target index
**Tags:** #core-inflation #trimmed-mean #headline-inflation #inflation-targeting

---

## Definition

A measure of inflation meant to be less sensitive to temporary price movements and to supply shocks (oil prices, for example), and more reflective of the underlying trend than the headline index. Minella et al. (2002) give the two common methods: **exclusion** (drop items such as food, oil by-products and other energy, which are seasonal and prone to supply shocks) and the **symmetric trimmed mean** (each period, drop the items with the largest and smallest price changes until a weight threshold is reached at each end, smoothing items adjusted only from time to time so they are not systematically excluded).

## Mechanism

Two distinct uses, which the sources keep apart:
- **As the target.** The argument for targeting core is that monetary policy should not chase a variable subject to temporary movements and should not react to supply shocks it cannot offset without output costs.
- **As information.** A core measure can predict the headline trend: if core Granger-causes headline but not the reverse, it carries information about future inflation beyond past inflation, and can guide policy without being the target.

## When it applies / when it fails

- **Against targeting core** (Minella et al.): it is less representative of the loss of purchasing power, since agents care about the whole basket; exclusion can leave out a large share of it (in Brazil, excluding administered items would drop more than 30% of the basket); a trimmed-mean basket is not known in advance and its threshold is arbitrary, so it is hard to communicate.
- **History matters for credibility**: where governments manipulated price indices (Brazil in the 1970s and 1980s), excluding items evokes those episodes, so a headline target is "essential for credibility reasons", at least at the start of a regime (Bogdanski, Tombini & Werlang; Minella et al.).
- International practice cited by Minella et al. had moved toward headline targets, with Canada and Thailand the exceptions targeting core by exclusion; an index excluding only mortgage interest (United Kingdom, South Africa) has a different motive.

## BRL / application to the current analysis

Minella et al. (2002) are about Brazil: the IPCA headline is the target, and the BCB's smoothed trimmed-mean core is used as a predictor of the headline trend rather than as the target. Bogdanski, Tombini & Werlang (2000) give the credibility reason for choosing headline at the start of the regime.
