# Debt Sustainability

**Type:** Fiscal solvency condition (debt dynamics)
**Tags:** #public-debt #debt-dynamics #r-minus-g #primary-balance

---

## Definition

Whether the government can service its debt without a large future break. **Solvency** is the narrower condition — the present value of current and future primary spending does not exceed that of current and future revenue, net of initial debt; **sustainability** adds realism: debt is sustainable if the government is solvent and can pay without being forced into a cut in consumption and living standards that may be economically or politically unfeasible (Conof-Conle, 2023, note 28).

## Debt dynamics

The ratio of debt to GDP moves with the primary balance and with the gap between the interest rate on the debt and the growth rate of the economy:

- The primary surplus that holds the ratio constant equals the debt ratio times the difference between the real interest rate and real growth. It is larger the higher the interest rate and the lower growth.
- While the primary balance stays below that level, debt and interest costs keep rising, and the rising debt itself raises the required surplus.
- The interest rate that matters is the one on the debt; a short-maturity, floating-rate debt transmits a rise in market rates to a large share of the stock quickly, so the same ratio is more fragile than in a country with long fixed-rate debt.

## When it applies / when it fails

- **The arithmetic is mechanical; the inputs are not.** The required surplus moves a lot with small changes in the assumed real rate and growth — Conof-Conle (2023, Table 1) show it changing sign within the plausible range.
- **Debt maturity and indexation change the speed** at which a rate shock reaches the debt, independently of the level (Conof-Conle, 2023, §§ 6–7, citing Giambiagi and Além).
- **Level effects are contested evidence, not a law.** Thresholds above which debt lowers growth or raises inflation come from cross-country studies cited secondhand in the linked note.
