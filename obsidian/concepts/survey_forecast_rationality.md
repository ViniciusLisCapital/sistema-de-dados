# Survey Forecast Rationality

**Type:** Empirical property of expectations / test
**Tags:** #inflation-expectations #forecast-bias #rationality #focus-survey

---

## Definition

Whether survey (or market) forecasts of inflation behave as rational expectations should: **unbiased** (realized inflation equals the forecast on average) and **efficient** (forecast errors cannot be predicted with information available when the forecast was made, including past errors). The standard test regresses realized inflation on the forecast and checks for a zero constant and a unit slope; weak rationality adds that errors are not serially correlated or predictable from past data (Gaglianone's survey; Vicente & Guillen apply the same regression to break-even inflation).

## Mechanism

- **Why tests reject.** Rejection is the norm in survey data and is what motivated models with information rigidities, under which errors are predictable even though each agent is rational given her information (Gaglianone).
- **Bias without irrationality.** A forecaster with an asymmetric loss over errors, a misspecified model, or strategic incentives (to stand out or to herd) can be biased while optimizing (Gaglianone, footnote 7).
- **Why a market forecast fails the same test.** For break-even inflation, a time-varying risk premium correlated with the forecast biases the slope away from one, and can turn it negative, without the market's expectation being irrational (Vicente & Guillen).

## When it applies / when it fails

- **Horizon is decisive.** In Brazil, unbiasedness of Focus forecasts is typically not rejected at 1 to 3 months and rejected at 6 and 12 months in some samples (studies surveyed by Gaglianone); break-even inflation is unbiased at 3 and 6 months and not at longer horizons (Vicente & Guillen).
- **Sample is decisive.** The same tests on the same survey give different answers in different periods (e.g. no bias at any horizon in 2004–2008 in one study surveyed by Gaglianone).
- **Who forecasts matters.** Professional forecasters in Brazil tend to underpredict, consumers to overpredict, and consensus forecasts of consumers fail rationality tests even when part of individual consumers pass (Gaglianone).
- Overlapping horizons make errors serially correlated by construction; tests need corrections (Newey–West, instruments) for it (Vicente & Guillen; Bevilaqua, Mesquita & Minella note that overlapping 12-month errors are highly correlated).

## BRL / application to the current analysis

All three sources are about Brazil: survey forecasts tend to underpredict at 6–12 months in several samples and to be unbiased at short horizons; break-even inflation forecasts well only at short horizons; forecasts formed in the 2002 crisis overestimated the inflation that followed.
