# International Risk Sharing

**Type:** Equilibrium condition (complete international asset markets)
**Tags:** #risk-sharing #complete-markets #open-economy #real-exchange-rate

---

## Definition

The condition that holds when households at home and abroad trade a complete set of state-contingent claims internationally: both price the same claims with their own marginal utility of consumption, so the ratio of home to foreign marginal utility is tied to the real exchange rate in every state. In the New Keynesian small open economy (Galí & Monacelli; Galí ch. 8) it links home consumption to world consumption (equal to world output) and the real exchange rate, up to a constant set by initial conditions.

## Mechanism

Combining the home and foreign Euler equations for the same nominal stochastic discount factor and iterating gives home consumption as world consumption times the real exchange rate raised to 1/σ. Since the real exchange rate is proportional to the terms of trade, a real depreciation goes with higher relative home consumption. In these models the condition does three things: with goods-market clearing it pins down how output, consumption and the terms of trade comove; combined with the Euler equations it delivers uncovered interest parity, which is therefore "not an additional independent equilibrium condition" (Galí & Monacelli); and it defines the consumption/output possibilities set of a planner, so the efficient allocation, and the case for domestic inflation targeting, rest on it.

## When it applies / when it fails

- It requires complete international markets, an assumption in both sources; Galí ch. 8 lists incomplete markets among the departures that can make strict domestic inflation targeting suboptimal.
- It implies a stationary terms of trade when the productivity differential is stationary (Galí & Monacelli, footnote 12), which feeds the stationarity of prices and exchange rates under all regimes they study — at odds with the data, as the authors note.
