# Economic Uncertainty (as a driver of the business cycle)

**Type:** Demand determinant / transmission channel to activity
**Tags:** #uncertainty #business-cycle #investment #is-curve

---

## Definition

The degree to which households and firms cannot foresee the economic environment — in the sources so far, measured by news-based economic policy uncertainty indices (Baker et al., 2016) or by the FGV's Indicador de Incerteza Econômica – Brasil (IIE-Br). Treated as a determinant of the cyclical component of activity, alongside the real interest rate and external demand.

## Mechanism

Higher uncertainty makes agents postpone decisions that are costly to reverse (BCB 2019, citing Forbes 2016):

- **Investment**: falls, most of all irreversible investment, construction and exporting sectors; productive capacity falls with it.
- **Consumption**: precautionary saving rises, with larger effects on durables and big-ticket goods.
- **Allocative efficiency**: more productive firms expand less, entry and exit are postponed, supply chains shorten — productivity growth slows.
- **Labour market**: firms hire, train and raise wages less and use more temporary workers; workers change jobs less.

In an IS-curve specification uncertainty enters as an additional term next to the real-rate gap and the world output gap; a common (global) factor and a country-specific factor can be separated.

## When it applies / when it fails

- Strongest where decisions are irreversible and expectation-driven: investment responds more than consumption or GDP (BCB 2019).
- Global uncertainty matters on its own and its effects can persist for long periods; high-uncertainty periods can slow a cyclical recovery (BCB 2019).
- Causality is not settled: activity can also drive uncertainty, and lagged instruments "may not fully solve" the endogeneity, so the estimates call for caution as causal effects (BCB 2019).
- Panel estimates are averages across countries; an individual country can differ in magnitude (BCB 2019).

## BRL / application to the current analysis

Brazil is in the BCB (2019) panel, but the study reports only pooled estimates and no Brazil-specific result; it notes that the BCB has been seeking to capture uncertainty in its macroeconomic models.
