# Sticky Wages (Nominal Wage Rigidity)

**Type:** Labor-market friction (modeling device)
**Tags:** #sticky-wages #wage-inflation #wage-markup #new-keynesian #calvo

---

## Definition

Nominal wages that adjust only infrequently to shocks. In the New Keynesian framework of Erceg, Henderson, and Levin (2000), as presented by Galí (ch. 6), workers supply differentiated labor types and have monopoly power over their wage, set by them or a union representing them; each period the wage of any given type can be reset only with probability 1 − θw, "independently of the time elapsed since they last adjusted their wage" — the Calvo device of price setting applied to wages. As a result "the aggregate nominal wage responds sluggishly to shocks, generating inefficient variations in the average wage markup".

## Mechanism

- **Wage setting**: a union resetting its wage chooses it as a markup over a weighted average of expected future marginal rates of substitution between consumption and work, plus expected prices — rising in expected prices "because households care about the purchasing power of their nominal wage". Without wage rigidity the real wage equals the desired gross wage markup times the marginal rate of substitution (Galí ch. 6, (11)–(12)).
- **Wage inflation equation**: aggregating gives wage inflation as a function of expected wage inflation and of the gap between the average wage markup and its desired level, with a slope that falls as wages get stickier (θw), as labor types become more substitutable (ϵw) and as labor supply becomes less elastic (ϕ) (Galí ch. 6, (15)). It replaces the condition that the real wage equals the marginal rate of substitution: the real wage "will not move one-for-one" with it.
- **Real wage gap**: written against the real wage gap (the real wage minus its natural level), price and wage inflation equations together with an identity linking the gap's change to wage inflation, price inflation and the change in the natural wage form the nonpolicy block (Galí ch. 6, (18), (20), (21)).
- **Welfare cost**: with staggered wage setting, wage inflation creates relative wage dispersion and an inefficient allocation of labor, symmetric to price dispersion under sticky prices; the welfare loss gains a wage inflation term whose weight rises with ϵw, with 1−α and with θw (Galí ch. 6, (28)–(29)).

## When it applies / when it fails

- With both sticky prices and sticky wages, zero price and wage inflation imply a constant real wage, so the flexible-price/flexible-wage allocation is unattainable whenever the natural real wage moves, as it does with technology shocks; strict price inflation targeting is then suboptimal except in the limit of fully flexible wages (Galí ch. 6).
- With demand shocks only the natural wage does not move, so stabilizing either inflation measure stabilizes everything (Galí ch. 6).
- In a calibrated exercise the joint sticky-price/sticky-wage model fits the estimated responses to a monetary shock better than either rigidity alone: with flexible wages the real wage falls by an "(implausibly) large" amount, with flexible prices price inflation drops sharply (Galí ch. 6, Figure 6.2, citing Christiano, Eichenbaum, and Evans 2005).
- The basic model has no wage indexation, which the medium-scale models cited by Galí (Smets and Wouters; Christiano, Eichenbaum, and Evans) add.
