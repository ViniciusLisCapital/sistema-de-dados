# Real-Time Data (Data Revisions and Output-Gap Mismeasurement)

**Type:** Measurement problem / information constraint on policy
**Tags:** #real-time-data #data-revisions #output-gap #policy-rules #measurement

---

## Definition

The data a policymaker actually had when a decision was made — preliminary releases, within-quarter forecasts and contemporaneous estimates of unobservables such as potential output — as opposed to the ex post revised data later used in research. Orphanides indexes each estimate by its vintage: the estimate of a variable for quarter t available in quarter t+i. For the current quarter, the "estimate" is itself a forecast.

## Mechanism

- **Rules need what is measured worst**: a Taylor-type rule needs nominal output, real output and potential output, none of which "is known with much accuracy until several quarters or perhaps years later"; output data are first available a month after the quarter and substantially revised, and potential-output revisions "tend to persist and even become amplified", with the estimate for the latest quarter "notoriously inaccurate, especially around business cycle turning points" (Orphanides).
- **Three consequences** (Orphanides): rules reacting to within-quarter data are not operational; a setting consistent with the rule at the time can look too easy or too tight after revisions, which changes the reading of historical episodes; and reaction functions estimated on revised data can be biased or misspecified, with residuals that mix policy surprises and data revisions.
- **Revisions are not only early**: in Orphanides' 1987-1992 sample the output-gap revision from three to four quarters after the quarter still has a standard deviation of 48 basis points, and revisions to real output explain most of the gap revisions. Rules on lagged data therefore face "the same informational problems", as "a matter of degree".

## When it applies / when it fails

- It matters most when the question is how policymakers did or should react to current information — precisely the purpose of reactive rules (Orphanides).
- Less-revised data are not immune: the policy-relevant unemployment measure is the gap to an uncertain natural rate, and CPI inflation needs an uncertain bias correction; fewer revisions may reflect "permanent mismeasurement" (Orphanides).
- The fix proposed is not to abandon rules but to evaluate them with explicit equations linking true and real-time variables, whose errors "cannot be assumed away" (Orphanides).
