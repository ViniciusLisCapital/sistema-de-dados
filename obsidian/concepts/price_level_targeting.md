# Price-Level Targeting

**Type:** Monetary policy regime / targeting rule
**Tags:** #price-level-targeting #history-dependence #zero-lower-bound #inflation-targeting

---

## Definition

A regime whose objective is to stabilize the price level around a price-level target, which need not be constant: it can follow a deterministic path corresponding to a steady inflation rate (Svensson). Unlike inflation targeting, past deviations of inflation from target are undone, so the price level is trend-stationary rather than nonstationary.

## Mechanism

- **Trend-stationary prices.** Under inflation targeting past misses are not reversed, which gives the price level a unit root and a conditional variance that grows without bound with the horizon; a price-level target makes that variance constant, so long-run price-level uncertainty is smaller (Svensson).
- **Automatic stabilization.** If the price level falls below a credible target, expected inflation rises and the real rate falls even with an unchanged nominal rate, pulling the price level back (Svensson); Eggertsson and Woodford call it an "automatic stabilizer": a larger shock causes more deflation and therefore more expected subsequent inflation.
- **At the zero lower bound.** Eggertsson and Woodford's optimal policy is history-dependent and can be implemented by a gap-adjusted price-level target (price level plus a multiple of the output gap) that is raised whenever the bound prevents hitting it; a simpler constant gap-adjusted target does almost as well, and neither needs an estimate of the natural rate.

## When it applies / when it fails

- Its case is strongest when the zero lower bound binds and the economy is in a liquidity trap (Svensson; Eggertsson and Woodford, with Japan as the motivating case).
- Communication is essential: Eggertsson and Woodford's differenced version of the simple rule gives the same equilibrium outside the trap but mandates deflation during the recovery and does worse than a strict zero-inflation target.
- A true price-level target also means undoing inflations with deflations, and whether it has negative real effects is still debated (Friedman's discussion of Eggertsson and Woodford; Svensson).
