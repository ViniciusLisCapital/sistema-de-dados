# Natural Rate of Interest

**Type:** Equilibrium benchmark / state variable
**Tags:** #natural-rate #r-star #new-keynesian #monetary-stance

---

## Definition

The real interest rate that would prevail in the flexible-price equilibrium (Galí: "the real interest rate that would prevail in the flexible price equilibrium"). It is exogenous to monetary policy and moves with real shocks — in the basic New Keynesian model, technology and preference (discount rate) shocks.

## Mechanism

In the dynamic IS equation the output gap depends on the gap between the actual real rate and the natural rate. Solved forward, the output gap is "inversely related to the sum of current and anticipated deviations between the real interest rate and its natural counterpart" (Galí). The stance of monetary policy is therefore measured against the natural rate, not against a fixed level: a shock that moves the natural rate (a preference shock, a technology shock) changes the gap even if the policy rate does not move, and a policy rate that falls can still be contractionary if it falls by less than the natural rate.

## When it applies / when it fails

- It is defined inside a model: its level depends on the model's shocks and parameters and it is not observed.
- In the basic NK model a rule that does not track movements in the natural rate leaves the output gap and inflation moving after real shocks (Galí's technology shock: the real rate falls, but not enough to match the natural rate).

| [[monetary_policy/synthesis/optimal_monetary_policy_inertia (Woodford, 1999)]] | Optimal policy posed as the optimal response to natural-rate disturbances, which summarize all non-monetary shocks; policy matters only through the interest-rate gap; the optimal rate adjusts partially toward a moving average of current and expected natural rates (3.35)–(3.37) |
| [[monetary_policy/synthesis/liquidity_trap_japan (Krugman, 1998)]] | The trap arises when the market-clearing real rate at full employment is negative; reasons it can be negative despite positive returns to capital (equity premium, expected fall in q from a shrinking workforce, nontradables in an open economy); Rogoff's comment doubts it is negative for Japan |
| [[monetary_policy/synthesis/natural_rate_international (Holston, Laubach & Williams, 2017)]] | Laubach–Williams model applied to the US, Canada, the Euro Area and the UK: r* fell in all four over 1990–2016, mostly through lower trend growth (less so in the Euro Area); the national estimates co-move, pointing to global factors; estimates are highly imprecise |
| [[monetary_policy/synthesis/neutral_rate_time_varying_brazil (Perrelli & Roache, 2014)]] | Brazil: an interval for a time-varying neutral rate from consumption models, filters, the yield curve, a state-space model and fundamentals regressions; a large fall over 2002–2013 driven by lower public debt and sovereign risk, financial deepening and lower global rates; short- and long-run equilibrium rates diverge |
| [[monetary_policy/synthesis/high_interest_rates_puzzle_brazil (Segura-Ubiergo, 2012)]] | Panel of 15 emerging-market inflation targeters, 1980–2009: real rates fall with domestic saving (public saving more than private), a stronger current account and lower inflation volatility, and rise with the fed funds rate; Brazil keeps an unexplained premium of about two percentage points |
| [[monetary_policy/synthesis/implementing_it_brazil (Bogdanski, Tombini & Werlang, 2000)]] | The BCB's IS curve re-calibrated so the long-run equilibrium real rate equals potential growth, because the peg-era sample implied a "necessarily high" one that should change with the new regime |
| [[monetary_policy/synthesis/credit_interest_jurisdictional_uncertainty (Arida, Bacha & Lara-Resende, 2005)]] | Brazil's high equilibrium rate attributed to jurisdictional uncertainty and to the policies it provoked: turnover taxes, forced saving and informality lower natural output and raise the rate needed to keep inflation on target; unwinding them shifts the domestic-balance curve and lowers the rate |
