# Natural Rate of Interest

**Type:** Equilibrium benchmark / state variable
**Tags:** #natural-rate #r-star #new-keynesian #monetary-stance

---

## Definition

The real interest rate that would prevail in the flexible-price equilibrium (Galí: "the real interest rate that would prevail in the flexible price equilibrium"). It is exogenous to monetary policy and moves with real shocks — in the basic New Keynesian model, technology and preference (discount rate) shocks.

## Mechanism

In the dynamic IS equation the output gap depends on the gap between the actual real rate and the natural rate. Solved forward, the output gap is "inversely related to the sum of current and anticipated deviations between the real interest rate and its natural counterpart" (Galí). The stance of monetary policy is therefore measured against the natural rate, not against a fixed level: a shock that moves the natural rate (a preference shock, a technology shock) changes the gap even if the policy rate does not move, and a policy rate that falls can still be contractionary if it falls by less than the natural rate.

## When it applies / when it fails

- It is defined inside a model: its level depends on the model's shocks and parameters and it is not observed.
- In the basic NK model a rule that does not track movements in the natural rate leaves the output gap and inflation moving after real shocks (Galí's technology shock: the real rate falls, but not enough to match the natural rate).
