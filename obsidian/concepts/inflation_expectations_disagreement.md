# Inflation Expectations Disagreement

**Type:** Property of survey expectations / proxy for inflation uncertainty
**Tags:** #inflation-expectations #disagreement #inflation-uncertainty #focus-survey

---

## Definition

The cross-sectional dispersion of point forecasts of inflation across survey respondents at a given date and horizon, measured by the standard deviation or the coefficient of variation (standard deviation over the mean). It is distinct from each forecaster's own uncertainty about the outcome; where a survey collects only point forecasts, as Brazil's Focus survey does, disagreement is the only uncertainty measure available (Bevilaqua, Mesquita & Minella).

## Mechanism

- **Why it exists at all.** Under full-information rational expectations every agent conditions on the same information and there is no disagreement. It arises from information frictions (agents who have not updated keep old forecasts; agents who update see noisy and private signals) and also, without any information friction, from different asymmetries in forecasters' loss functions (Gaglianone, footnote 7).
- **Why it tracks uncertainty.** In the US Survey of Professional Forecasters, disagreement moves with the average individual uncertainty read from probability histograms, which is what justifies using it as an uncertainty proxy where only point forecasts exist (Giordani and Söderlind, as cited by Bevilaqua, Mesquita & Minella and Gaglianone).
- **Why it relates to credibility.** When the target is credible, forecasters share a focal point, so dispersion falls; when it is doubted, beliefs about future policy diverge (Sicsú, as surveyed by Gaglianone).

## When it applies / when it fails

- As an uncertainty measure it is a proxy, valid to the extent disagreement co-moves with individual uncertainty; that co-movement is documented for the US, not tested for Brazil (Bevilaqua, Mesquita & Minella).
- In Brazil it is persistent and rises with the country risk premium and with inflation (for consumers) or the change in inflation (for professionals), and falls with output growth (studies surveyed by Gaglianone). It soared in the 2002 confidence crisis and fell after 2003 (Bevilaqua, Mesquita & Minella).
- Because bias and disagreement can come from loss-function asymmetries, high disagreement does not by itself identify information rigidity (Gaglianone).

## BRL / application to the current analysis

Three of the four linked sources are about Brazil's Focus survey (and, in Gaglianone, the FGV consumer survey), and they agree: disagreement rose sharply in the 2002 confidence crisis and fell after 2003 (Bevilaqua, Mesquita & Minella), and it moves with country risk and with rising inflation — Carvalho & Minella's regressions on lagged EMBI and the change in 12-month inflation, and the studies Gaglianone surveys. Carvalho & Minella also find the dispersion of inflation, Selic and exchange-rate forecasts moving together, so Focus disagreement on inflation is not an isolated signal. That disagreement measures uncertainty is assumed from US evidence and not tested for Brazil. Coibion, Gorodnichenko & Kamdar is about the US and New Zealand: there disagreement is much larger among households and firms than among professionals, a warning that the professional Focus panel shows the low end of the dispersion in an economy. Nothing in the sources covers the period after their samples.
