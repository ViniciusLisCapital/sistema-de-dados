# Financial Accelerator

**Type:** Propagation mechanism (credit-market friction)
**Tags:** #financial-accelerator #external-finance-premium #net-worth #balance-sheet-channel #credit-frictions

---

## Definition

The amplification and propagation of shocks by endogenous developments in credit markets (Bernanke, Gertler & Gilchrist). Its core is an inverse relation between the **external finance premium** — the difference between the cost of funds raised externally and the opportunity cost of internal funds — and the borrower's **net worth** (liquid assets plus the collateral value of illiquid assets, less outstanding obligations). Because net worth is procyclical, the premium is countercyclical, and swings in borrowing, investment and production are larger and longer than they would be with frictionless credit.

## Mechanism

- **Why net worth lowers the premium.** With asymmetric information and agency costs, a borrower who contributes little own wealth has more divergent interests from lenders; lenders must be compensated for the higher expected agency (in the model, bankruptcy and monitoring) costs. In Bernanke, Gertler & Gilchrist this comes from Townsend's costly state verification: the optimal contract is risky debt, and capital demand is proportional to net worth with a factor increasing in the expected return over the safe rate.
- **Leverage magnifies asset-price surprises.** An unexpected change in the return to capital changes entrepreneurial equity by the ratio of capital holdings to equity, more than one-for-one when firms are leveraged.
- **The multiplier.** A shock that raises investment raises the price of capital, which raises net worth, lowers the premium and raises investment further; net worth reverts slowly, so the premium stays away from steady state and effects persist.
- **Monetary policy.** A policy easing lowers the premium through higher asset prices and net worth, so real effects are larger and more persistent than the rate change alone implies; conversely, smaller countercyclical rate moves are needed to dampen output.

## When it applies / when it fails

- It disappears without credit-market frictions: investment then goes to the point where the expected return on capital equals the riskless rate.
- Its strength rises with leverage and with how directly borrowers hold the assets whose prices move: amplification is larger when borrowers own the capital stock than when the friction sits only with capital producers (the comparison with Carlstrom & Fuerst).
- Firms with poorer access to credit respond more; the effect is not limited to the whole economy being constrained.
- The more policy stabilizes output, the smaller the role of the accelerator, as for any propagation mechanism.

## BRL / application to the current analysis

The linked sources say nothing about Brazil. Bernanke, Gertler & Gilchrist note that currency crises with foreign-currency loans redistribute wealth away from domestic borrowers when the exchange rate collapses, and leave the open-economy extension to future work.
