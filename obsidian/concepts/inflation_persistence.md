# Inflation Persistence (Inertia)

**Type:** Property of inflation dynamics
**Tags:** #inflation-persistence #inertia #backward-looking #phillips-curve #inflation-targeting

---

## Definition

The degree to which current inflation depends on past inflation — measured, in the sources linked here, by the coefficients on lagged inflation in a backward-looking aggregate supply (Phillips) curve. It arises when a share of firms set prices looking backward, or when contracts index prices to past inflation; it is called inertia when the focus is the part of this year's inflation inherited from last year.

## Mechanism

- **Why a regime can change it.** If inflation targeting changes how expectations are formed, fewer firms may set prices backward or they may weight past inflation less, so persistence falls (Minella et al., following Kuttner and Posen).
- **Why it matters for policy.** Lower persistence means a lower output cost of curbing inflationary pressure and of lowering average inflation. It also changes measured magnitudes elsewhere: with less persistence, the same exchange-rate or unemployment coefficient produces a smaller cumulative effect on inflation over a year, so a fall in measured 12-month pass-through can be a consequence of lower persistence rather than of a lower pass-through coefficient.
- **Inertia in policy practice.** The BCB separates the inertia inherited from the previous year from new shocks: policy should neutralize propagation, so inertia is offset over time (the BCB's rule of thumb is 18 months), and only part of it is accommodated because output volatility also counts (Minella et al. 2002, 2003).
- **Indexation as a source.** Contractual indexation to past price indices makes some prices (in Brazil, administered prices) more backward-looking than others, and strong backward-looking behavior makes disinflation slower and costlier, especially with declining targets.

## When it applies / when it fails

- Estimates are fragile over short samples: in Brazil the fall in persistence under inflation targeting shows up in a regression with interaction dummies but not in a recursive estimate, and a time-varying estimate rebounds in the last months of 2002 (Minella et al., 2003).
- Persistence can return when credibility is questioned: in late 2002 the weight on past inflation in market expectations rose as agents assigned some probability to a less strict policy under the next government (Minella et al., 2003).
- A purely forward-looking Phillips curve has no intrinsic persistence; see [[nk_phillips_curve]] for the hybrid curves that add lagged inflation.

## BRL / application to the current analysis

Both sources are about Brazil, 1994–2002: inflation persistence fell after inflation targeting was adopted in 1999, by the dummy estimates, and rose again in the 2002 confidence crisis. They say nothing about later periods.
