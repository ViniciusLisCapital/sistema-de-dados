# Information Rigidities

**Type:** Expectations-formation friction
**Tags:** #information-rigidities #sticky-information #noisy-information #inflation-expectations

---

## Definition

Departures from full-information rational expectations in which agents form expectations rationally given what they know, but do not know everything that is available: either they update their information only occasionally (sticky information) or they observe the state of the economy only through noisy signals (noisy information). They explain two features of survey data that full information cannot: disagreement among forecasters and predictable forecast errors (Gaglianone).

## Mechanism

- **Sticky information** (Mankiw and Reis; Reis, as summarized by Gaglianone). Each period an agent obtains new information with a constant probability, the degree of attention; when she does, she forms expectations with full information, otherwise she keeps her old forecast. One minus that probability is the degree of information rigidity. Updaters share the same forecast; non-updaters do not revise, so disagreement and slow adjustment of the average forecast follow.
- **Noisy information** (Woodford; Sims; Maćkowiak and Wiederholt, as summarized by Gaglianone). Agents update every period but see only a noisy signal, so the new forecast is a weighted average of the signal and the previous belief; the weight on the previous belief is the rigidity.
- **Strategic interaction** (Morris and Shin, in Gaglianone's survey). Forecasters weigh public against private signals; more precise information gets more weight, and forecasters may overweight private information to differentiate themselves.
- **Why it matters for macro dynamics.** Rigid information makes the average expectation respond gradually to news, which changes macro dynamics and the appropriate policy response (Gaglianone).

## When it applies / when it fails

- The Brazilian Focus evidence surveyed by Gaglianone fits it: forecasters do not update every period, those who update still disagree, updating is more frequent when incentives (the Top5 contest) reward attention, and sticky information explains median expectations better than rational or adaptive expectations in one study. A hybrid sticky-plus-noisy model fits only with more rigidity than observed.
- It is not the only explanation of bias and disagreement: asymmetric loss functions, model misspecification and strategic incentives produce them too (Gaglianone, footnote 7). Which framework describes reality best is "still a matter of intense debate".

## BRL / application to the current analysis

Two of the three linked sources are Brazilian. In Gaglianone's survey, Focus forecasters update infrequently, more so outside the windows that count for the Top5 ranking, and consumers in the FGV survey adjust essentially to current inflation. Areosa estimates a noisy-information model on Focus micro data: public information is more precise and weighs more than private, yet forecasters give it less weight than its precision warrants, which Areosa reads as strategic substitutability — forecasters differentiating themselves, possibly to enter the Top 5 ranking. Coibion, Gorodnichenko & Kamdar is US evidence: mean forecasts under-react to shocks and errors are predictable from revisions, with large implied rigidity; it says nothing about Brazil.
