# Inflation Expectations Anchoring

**Type:** Property of expectations formation / channel of a monetary regime
**Tags:** #inflation-expectations #anchoring #credibility #inflation-targeting

---

## Definition

Inflation expectations are anchored when they stay close to the central bank's target and do not respond to news, current shocks or realized inflation. The measurable signature in the sources linked here is a weak link: between long-run inflation compensation and macroeconomic news (Gürkaynak, Levin and Swanson, as surveyed by Blinder et al.), or between expected inflation and lagged inflation, the output gap and the exchange rate (Alves & Areosa).

## Mechanism

- **Through price setting.** In Alves & Areosa's model, firms that do not reoptimize their price index it to a weighted average of the official target and lagged inflation. Solving the Phillips curve forward, expected inflation depends on lagged inflation, the target path, the current output gap and real exchange-rate change, and expected future conditions. The higher the weight on the target, the less current demand and exchange-rate shocks and past inflation move expected inflation; at a weight of one they drop out and expectations depend only on the predetermined targets and expected future conditions, which move smoothly.
- **Through communication.** In Blinder et al.'s framework, expectations respond to central bank signals; an announced target is "long-run" communication, and anchoring is its measurable effect.
- **Why it matters.** With anchored expectations a supply or exchange-rate shock moves inflation once instead of feeding into expectations and persistence, so the policy response and its output cost are smaller.

## When it applies / when it fails

- **Anchoring is earned and can be lost.** In Brazil the estimated weight on the target fell sharply in the second half of 2002, and 12-month survey expectations left the target from September 2002 to July 2003 before returning; Alves & Areosa associate the recovery with the central bank's commitment and fiscal support, without testing it.
- **The target weight is not a pure credibility measure** (Alves & Areosa): in a time-invariant model it misses policy and state dependence, the lag between policy and its effects can lower it even under full credibility, and fiscal policy is left out.
- **Inflation targeting is not the only route** (Blinder et al.): expectations are found anchored across regimes in some studies, and the evidence that a target lowers inflation itself is much weaker than the evidence on anchoring.

## BRL / application to the current analysis

Alves & Areosa are about Brazil 1999–2004: the target weight was large on average, collapsed in the 2002 confidence crisis and recovered by the end of 2004, and survey expectations followed the same pattern. Blinder et al. say nothing about Brazil.
