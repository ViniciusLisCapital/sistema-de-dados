# Financial Trilemma

**Type:** Policy constraint (financial stability in open economies)
**Tags:** #financial-trilemma #financial-stability #financial-integration #macroprudential

---

## Definition

The proposition, formulated by Schoenmaker (2013) and used by Obstfeld, that only two of three can be enjoyed simultaneously: (1) national control over financial policies, (2) financial integration with the global market, (3) financial stability. It is distinct from the monetary trilemma (fixed exchange rate, free capital mobility, independent monetary policy), and capital movements are the link between the two (Issing).

## Mechanism

National prudential rules lose force when activity can move outside their reach: restrictions on domestic lenders do little "if foreign lenders can enter the market and operate without restriction", and limits on residents' foreign currency borrowing are weaker if the same firms can issue foreign currency debt offshore (Obstfeld). Reliance of financial insurance and resolution on the national budget can also segment markets along national lines and requires precautionary fiscal space. Because prudential tools are degraded, monetary policy must carry more of the financial stability burden, and its trade-off between macro stabilization and financial stability worsens.

## When it applies / when it fails

- It binds most in fully open financial systems; countries that keep capital controls (Ito's example of China, Thailand and other Asian EMEs) can retain national financial policy and stability.
- International regulatory cooperation softens it: Basel III's jurisdictional reciprocity for countercyclical capital buffers extends a country's buffer to foreign banks lending into it (Obstfeld).
- Issing calls it "de facto, a dilemma" between integration and stability and argues integration can in some circumstances foster stability; Ito finds its cases hard to illustrate, and under the "separation hypothesis" (effective macroprudential policy leaves monetary policy free to pursue flexible inflation targeting) it would matter less.
