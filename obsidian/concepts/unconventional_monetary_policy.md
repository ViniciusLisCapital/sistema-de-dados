# Unconventional Monetary Policy

**Type:** Policy instrument (central bank balance sheet)
**Tags:** #unconventional-monetary-policy #credit-policy #central-bank-balance-sheet #financial-crisis

---

## Definition

Monetary policy conducted through the size and composition of the central bank's balance sheet rather than through the short-term policy rate. In Gertler & Karadi its essence is "direct central bank intermediation of private lending": the central bank borrows from savers (issuing riskless government debt) and lends to private borrowers, directly or through private intermediaries, as the Federal Reserve did after August 2007 (wider discount-window collateral, commercial paper backstops, purchases of agency debt and mortgage-backed securities).

## Mechanism

- **Why it can work.** When private intermediaries' leverage is limited by their equity (an agency problem with depositors), a loss of intermediary capital cuts credit and raises spreads. The central bank, which "can commit to always honoring its debt", faces no such constraint, so it can replace part of private intermediation; total leverage of the system rises with the share it funds (Gertler & Karadi).
- **Transmission.** Central bank lending damps the rise in the spread between the return on capital and the riskless rate, which damps the fall in investment and asset prices.
- **Cost.** Public intermediation is less efficient than private: an efficiency cost per unit lent (raising funds via government debt, identifying good investments). The net gain depends on that cost.

## When it applies / when it fails

- It matters when private intermediation is balance-sheet constrained; with frictionless finance there is nothing for it to offset.
- In Gertler & Karadi's calibrated crisis the welfare gain falls to zero as the efficiency cost approaches forty basis points; the gain from an aggressive intervention over a moderate one is small.
- Equity injections into intermediaries are equivalent if efficiency costs are ignored; direct lending suits assets cheap for the central bank to hold (securitized high-grade assets), capital injections suit loans that need monitoring (C&I loans).
- The context is the zero lower bound, when the policy rate can no longer be lowered.
