# Divine Coincidence

**Type:** Property of optimal policy in the New Keynesian model
**Tags:** #divine-coincidence #optimal-policy #new-keynesian #cost-push

---

## Definition

The property that, in the basic New Keynesian model with an efficient natural allocation, stabilizing inflation and stabilizing the welfare-relevant output gap are the same objective: zero inflation implies output at its efficient level, and vice versa. Galí: the central bank "doesn't need to know or worry about what the efficient level of output is at each point in time, for the latter can be attained automatically, as a byproduct of a successful price stabilization policy" (he cites Blanchard and Galí 2007 for the term).

## Mechanism

If the price level is stable, no firm adjusts its price even when it can, so the price-setting constraints do not bind and the allocation is the flexible-price one — efficient once an employment subsidy removes the monopolistic-competition distortion (Galí ch. 4). In the New Keynesian Phillips curve without a cost-push shock, inflation depends only on current and expected output gaps, so closing the gap every period delivers zero inflation (Clarida, Galí & Gertler: the central bank "is able to hit its inflation and output targets simultaneously, all the time").

## When it applies / when it fails

- It fails when the Phillips curve carries a cost-push shock — anything that moves expected marginal cost independently of the output gap. Then a short-run tradeoff between inflation and output-gap variability appears, and optimal policy brings inflation back to target gradually (Clarida, Galí & Gertler, Results 1–2).
- It depends on the natural allocation being efficient; the inefficient case is treated in Galí's chapter 5.
- It concerns the output *gap*: output itself should move one-for-one with natural output, so rules that stabilize output around a smooth trend are suboptimal (Galí ch. 4).

| Source | Role |
|---|---|
| [[monetary_policy/synthesis/monetary_policy_design_nk_ch4 (Gali, 2015)]] | Names it: with an efficient natural allocation, optimal policy sets inflation and the output gap to zero at all times, and price stability delivers efficient output |
| [[monetary_policy/synthesis/science_of_monetary_policy (Clarida, Gali & Gertler, 1999)]] | Shows what breaks it: the tradeoff "emerges only if cost push inflation is present" (Result 1), which is why optimal inflation targeting is gradual |
| [[monetary_policy/synthesis/discretion_vs_commitment_ch5 (Gali, 2015)]] | Treats the case where it fails: a time-varying gap between natural and efficient output enters the NKPC as a cost-push shock, and optimal policy under discretion and commitment trades inflation against the output gap |
| [[monetary_policy/synthesis/sticky_wages_prices_ch6 (Gali, 2015)]] | Fails in general with sticky wages and prices (zero price and wage inflation fix the real wage), but obtains for composite inflation in a special parameter case and under demand shocks |
| [[monetary_policy/synthesis/open_economy_monetary_policy_ch8 (Gali, 2015)]] | Open-economy version: with σ = η = 1, no preference shocks and a suitable subsidy, stabilizing domestic inflation closes the output gap, so strict domestic inflation targeting is optimal |
| [[monetary_policy/synthesis/nk_small_open_economy (Gali & Monacelli, 2005)]] | The original small open economy result: with σ = η = 1 and an employment subsidy offsetting market power and the terms-of-trade incentive, domestic inflation targeting replicates the efficient allocation; CPI targeting and a peg lose welfare |
