# Global Financial Cycle

**Type:** Empirical regularity / international transmission channel
**Tags:** #global-financial-cycle #capital-flows #vix #risk-appetite #us-monetary-policy #spillovers

---

## Definition

The common movement, across countries and asset classes, of gross capital flows, credit growth, financial-sector leverage and risky asset prices, closely (negatively) correlated with the VIX — a measure of uncertainty and market risk aversion. Rey: "There is a global financial cycle in capital flows, asset prices and in credit growth", and it "is not aligned with countries' specific macroeconomic conditions".

## Mechanism

Monetary conditions in the centre country (the US) set the dollar funding cost of global banks. Looser policy lowers the VIX with a lag, raises the leverage of global (notably European) banks and cross-border credit flows, and raises credit growth; rising credit and asset prices compress spreads and lower measured risk, which relaxes value-at-risk constraints and allows more lending — a positive feedback loop. Because the transmission runs through gross credit flows and intermediaries' balance sheets rather than through the exchange rate, a floating rate does not shut it off (Rey).

## When it applies / when it fails

- It concerns gross flows; net flows show no common pattern, and FDI does not follow the cycle (Rey).
- It is strongest for credit and portfolio-debt flows; markets receiving more credit inflows are more sensitive to it, those with more equity flows less so (Rey, Table 2(a)).
- Monetary policy in the centre is "only one of the drivers"; Rey's own VAR attributes a modest-to-sizable share of VIX variance to Fed funds shocks depending on the specification.

| Source | Role |

| [[monetary_policy/synthesis/dilemma_not_trilemma (Rey, 2013)]] | Documents the cycle in flows (1990Q1–2012Q4), credit, leverage and a global factor in 858 risky asset prices; a recursive VAR links it to the Fed funds rate; concludes the trilemma becomes a "dilemma" |
| [[monetary_policy/synthesis/trilemma_tradeoffs (Obstfeld, 2015)]] | Accepts a financial cycle distinct from monetary cycles, transmitted via risk and term premia, borrowing constraints and offshore dollar credit; the VIX change significantly moves EME long-term rates; but floating still buys short-rate independence |
| [[monetary_policy/synthesis/us_policy_international_risk_spillovers (Kalemli-Ozcan, 2019)]] | Recasts US spillovers as risk spillovers through EME country risk premia (EMBI) and imperfect policy pass-through; argues, against Rey, that free floats are much better insulated than managed floats |
| [[monetary_policy/synthesis/us_monetary_policy_global_cycle (Miranda-Agrippino & Rey, 2020)]] | Identifies US monetary shocks with high-frequency external instruments in a Bayesian VAR: a tightening lowers the global factor in 858 risky asset prices, raises aggregate risk aversion by over 50% above trend, deleverages global banks and contracts global credit and cross-border flows, also for floaters |
| [[monetary_policy/synthesis/it_and_debt_brazil (Favero & Giavazzi, 2004)]] | Calvo's global factor in emerging-market spreads, proxied by the US BAA corporate spread: Brazil's EMBI follows it, but with a sensitivity that rises non-linearly as domestic fiscal fundamentals weaken (Brazil 1999–2004) |

## BRL / application to the current analysis

Rey's flow and panel data include Brazil, and she cites "the 2010 and 2011 Brazilian taxes on equity inflows" as an instance of temporary capital controls used against the cycle; she draws no Brazil-specific result.
