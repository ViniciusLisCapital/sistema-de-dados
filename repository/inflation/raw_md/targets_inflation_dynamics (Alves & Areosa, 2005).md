Abstract
Brazil has experienced crucial changes in its in‡ation process since the adoption of in‡ation
targeting in mid 1999. This article addresses changes in the analytical framework employed to
track the in‡ation dynamics, speci…cally the relevance of an explicit target for in‡ation. A New-
Keynesian Phillips curve (NKPC) is derived incorporating indexation not only to past in‡ation
but also to in‡ation targets, generalizing the Woodford (2003) hybrid curve.
In our modeling, …rms that do not optimally set their prices in a given period adjust them only
by indexing their previous prices to a weighted average of the in‡ation target and lagged in‡ation.
In such a framework, the impact of in‡ation targets on agents’ decisions regarding the supply
side can be analytically measured by the parameter associated to the in‡ation target. It is shown
that in‡ation target a¤ects the welfare-based monetary policy objective function by penalizing
deviations of actual in‡ation from target instead of from zero. This result establishes the micro
foundation basis for ad-hoc loss functions as indicated in traditional literature. Therefore, the
in‡ation target also a¤ects the optimal target criterion.
We also present a microfounded speci…cation to model in‡ation expectations, and conclude that
when …rms attribute a high weight to the government’s in‡ation target when setting their own
prices, exchange rate and demand shocks are unable to alter signi…cantly in‡ation expectations.
Such a result gives some light to empirical ad hoc assessment conducted in traditional literature.
Our empirical evidence shows that even after major shocks, the target ability of anchoring
in‡ation has been restored. Although not formally tested, such a fact followed the monetary au-
thorities’…rm commitment to meet the in‡ation targets, reinforced by the government’s necessary
support through a consistent …scal policy.

Introduction
What are the consequences of adopting an explicit target for in‡ation? Since the early 1990s,
a growing number of central banks in industrial and emerging countries have considered this
question, including the US1.
The in‡ation targeting literature points out that much of its bene…ts can be attributed
to its impact on in‡ation expectations2. Woodford (2004) argues that the most important
achievement of in‡ation-targeting central banks has not been the reorientation of the goals of
monetary policy toward a stronger emphasis on controlling in‡ation, but rather the development
of an approach to the conduct of policy that focuses on a clearly de…ned target. According to
him, one important advantage of commitment to an appropriately chosen policy rule is that it
facilitates public understanding of policy, which is crucial in order for monetary policy to be
most e¤ective3.
This seems to be the case in Brazil. As pointed out by Cerisola and Gelos (2005), the
adoption of an explicit and public target for in‡ation in‡uenced the expectations of private
agents. The authors examine the macroeconomic determinants of survey in‡ation expectations
in Brazil since the adoption of in‡ation targeting in 1999. The results suggest that the in‡ation
targeting framework has helped anchor expectations, with the dispersion of in‡ation expecta-
tions declining considerably. They also …nd that the in‡ation target has been instrumental in
shaping expectations while the importance of past in‡ation in determining expectations appears
to be relatively low.
The success of in‡ation targeting is solidly based on credibility. Private agents must believe
that the central bank will act consistently within the in‡ation targeting framework. During the
process of credibility construction in the context of large shocks, however, even a strong response
by the monetary authority, will not be able to prevent expectations to deviate from the targets.
In this case communication with the public so as to explain the reasons of the breach becomes
crucial. Furthermore, it is important that expectations converge to the target over a certain
time horizon. Minella et al (2003) presented some evidence on the behavior of the Central
Bank of Brazil, private agents’expectations, and changes in in‡ation dynamics. The authors
conclude that the Central Bank of Brazil has been reacting strongly to expected in‡ation. It
conducts monetary policy in a forward-looking manner, and responds to in‡ationary pressures.
Although the actual in‡ation has been above the upper limit of the tolerance interval in 2001
and 2002, the in‡ation-targeting regime has been successful in anchoring expectations, with a
substantial reduction in the degree of in‡ation persistence after in‡ation targeting was adopted.
This is a direct consequence of credibility gains that the Central Bank of Brazil has obtained
since the implementation of the new regime. Credibility, however, is still under construction as
it takes time to achieve and great e¤ort to preserve.

In‡ation targeting in Brazil
Soon after changing to a ‡oating exchange rate regime in 1999, Brazil has adopted an explicit
in‡ation targeting framework as part of an extensive program of economic reforms. This devel-
opment ended a period during which the exchange rate had been the main anchor for monetary
policy4. In June 1999, a presidential decree established the new monetary framework, whose
key points are listed bellow:
 Adoption of an explicit target for in‡ation;
 In‡ation targets as well as the respective tolerance intervals were to be set by the National
Monetary Council on the basis of a proposal by the Minister of Finance5;
 Targets to be set no later than June 30, two years in advance;
 The Central Bank was given the responsibility to implement the policies necessary to

plementation of a more strict in‡ation targeting framework, one that would represent a …rm
commitment to prevent in‡ation from getting out of control. Also, the relatively loose …scal

Central Bank presented additional challenges to the conduct of monetary policy, in particular
to the construction of credibility. In order to deal with these concerns, the Central Bank has
adopted a ‡exible and accountable approach in conducting policy. For instance, even when the

sidering adopting in‡ation targeting: the way the Central Bank articulated the reasons why
the initial in‡ation target was missed, how it responded to the shock, and how it planned to
return to its longer-run in‡ation goal.

the intensity and frequency of shocks being unprecedented. Despite challenging conditions the

current in‡ation deviated from the established targets, monetary policy under in‡ation target
was, most of the times, capable of keeping in‡ation expectations in line with the o¢ cial in‡ation

We develop and estimate a structural model of in‡ation to measure the relevance of an explicit
target for in‡ation. A New-Keynesian Phillips curve (NKPC) is derived incorporating in‡ation
targets generalizing the Woodford’s (2003) hybrid curve.

target instead of from zero. This result establishes the micro foundation basis for ad-hoc loss
functions as indicated in the traditional literature. Therefore, the o¢ cial in‡ation target also

IPEA and IBGE released annual GDP (1970, 1975, 1980, 1985, 1996, 1999, 2000, 2001
and 2002) from several cities in Brazil.
In particular, we could aggregate GDP from São
Paulo’s metropolitan region29 and determine its share to Brazilian GDP, for each year. Since
this indicator are not supposed to present a volatile pattern from a particular quarter to the
following one, except from seasonal idiosyncrasies, we generated quarterly shares minimizing the
sum of squared second di¤erences constrained to the restriction that their annual average should
equal the previously estimated occurred shares for each one of the cited years. Following, we
applied this estimated quarterly shares to actual seasonally adjusted quarterly Brazilian GDP.
For years 2003, 2004 and 2005, for which such cities GDP were not released, we considered the
